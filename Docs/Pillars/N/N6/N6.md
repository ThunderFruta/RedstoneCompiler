# N6 — Selective stale-result salvage

**Status:** necessary requirement.

**Primary owner:** `PhysicalDesign/Orchestration/`.

## Governing rule

> A result computed against an older design snapshot may be reused only when
> the coordinator proves that every dependency relevant to the reused claim is
> unchanged, or that each intervening change is provably non-interfering with
> that claim's declared scope.

Every result produced against a non-current snapshot is stale by default and
is ineligible for ranking, proof propagation, cache publication, or commitment
until the coordinator certifies its exact reusable claims.

## Required process

1. Classify every snapshot mismatch as `StaleUnreviewed`.
2. Identify the exact claim proposed for reuse.
3. Load its immutable claim-specific dependency manifest.
4. Compare every relevant dependency against the change set.
5. Establish exact equality, scoped non-interference, or a recognized
   monotonicity proof for every dependency.
6. Reject the claim when dependency coverage is incomplete or uncertain.
7. Preserve the old production snapshot and result provenance.
8. Issue a separate coordinator-owned reuse certificate against the current
   snapshot.
9. Revalidate the certified claim under ordinary current-snapshot checks.
10. Permit ranking or commitment only after that revalidation.

Formally, a claim `C` from `S_old` may be reused against `S_new` only when, for
every `d` in `Dependencies(C)`, at least one condition holds:

```text
Equal(d, S_old, S_new)
or ProvenNonInterference(ChangeSet(S_old, S_new), d, Scope(C))
or ProvenMonotonicPreservation(ChangeSet(S_old, S_new), C)
```

The coordinator then issues a reuse certificate for `C` against `S_new`.

## Non-proofs

The following do not establish non-interference:

- The change is outside the old occupied bounding box.
- Cell coordinates did not change.
- The circuit, signal, task, placement, or cache key has the same name.
- The old result was expensive.
- The worker already finished.
- The result passed under an older model or policy version.
- No conflict was noticed during partial inspection.

## Claim strength

Salvage may retain or weaken a claim but never strengthen it:

- Feasible remains feasible, not optimal.
- A candidate remains a candidate, not accepted.
- A partial search remains incomplete.
- A local proof remains locally scoped.
- An old optimum loses optimality unless the full domain and objective remain
  equivalent.
- An old infeasibility proof survives only when its exhausted domain remains
  covered and no applicable choice was added or constraint relaxed.

## Claim-specific requirements

| Old claim | Minimum salvage requirement |
|---|---|
| Static cell geometry | Same template, transform semantics, model compatibility, and orientation |
| Placement occupancy | Same transforms and no new occupancy, support, air, or influence conflict |
| Pin-access feasibility | Same roles and access geometry with all relevant obstacles, reservations, corridors, and capacities accounted for |
| Local feasible route | Same endpoints and directed behavior; all route, support, repeater, ownership, and influence claims remain legal |
| Global assignment | Every selected subresult remains valid and all shared capacity, portal, lease, crossing, and ownership constraints remain compatible |
| Metric value | Every metric input and its definition version remain valid; otherwise recompute it |
| Relative ranking | Every compared candidate's relevant metrics and objective version remain valid; otherwise rerank |
| Optimality proof | Complete candidate domain, constraints, objective, tie-breaking, and completeness evidence remain equivalent |
| Infeasibility proof | The proven domain remains exhausted and was not expanded or relaxed, except through a certified monotonic preservation rule |
| No-good | Its exact assumptions and scope still hold; it never widens itself to new orientations or domain members |
| Incomplete frontier | Retain only individually valid nodes and never infer completeness from a partially salvaged frontier |

## Opportunistic scheduling

Stale-result review normally runs as opportunistic R6 work. The active pipeline
does not wait solely for speculative salvage.

The coordinator may promote a salvage task only when it can advance a current
required decision, is expected to cost less than replacement work, has a
current review contract, and receives a bounded critical budget.

On demotion, a salvage task may pause or retain bounded immutable checkpoints.
On termination, the task instance performs no more review; continuing requires
a new task identity and current dependency contract. Unfinished claim reviews
remain `StaleUnreviewed` in either case.

Partial comparison progress proves nothing. Completed dependency
classifications may be checkpointed only when they are immutable, provenance
complete, and cheap to retain. Resumption first checks whether the intended
current snapshot is still current.

## Demotion and termination

- Demotion lowers priority but leaves the salvage task live or resumable.
- Termination ends that task instance and releases every live task resource.
- Neither operation certifies or rejects the stale claim.
- Cancellation is a request; termination is the completed lifecycle state.
- Completed certified subclaims may survive as immutable artifacts.
- An incomplete inspection never becomes a negative proof.

See [R6](../../R/R6/R6.md) for the complete scheduler and lifecycle contract.

## Required provenance

A certified salvage record contains:

```text
ProducedAgainstSnapshotFingerprint
ValidatedAgainstSnapshotFingerprint
DependencyManifestFingerprint
ChangeSetFingerprint
SalvagedClaimFingerprints
RevalidatedClaimFingerprints
NonInterferenceProofFingerprints
ReuseCertificateFingerprint
```

The original result is never relabeled as though it were computed against the
new snapshot.

## Direct identity validation remains strict

The ordinary request/result identity validator continues to reject mismatched
snapshot or dependency fingerprints. N6 is a separate coordinator operation;
it must not be implemented as `AllowStale=True` on the direct commit gate.

The permitted flow is:

```text
old result
    -> StaleUnreviewed
    -> coordinator salvage review
        -> SalvageRejected
        or reuse certificate
    -> current-snapshot revalidation
    -> ordinary ranking and commitment eligibility
```

## Acceptance

- Exact-current results use the normal strict fast path.
- Every stale result is rejected before review.
- Positive and negative non-interference cases are covered.
- Partial salvage cannot change accepted state.
- Demotion, termination, deadline, or worker failure cannot certify a claim.
- Fresh and salvaged equivalent work produce identical physical behavior.
- Worker order does not change certification or commitment.
- Telemetry reports review work, preemption, checkpoint cost, rejected claims,
  revalidation cost, and recomputation avoided.
