# R2 — Choose placement and routing space together

**Status:** target behavior.

**Primary owner:** `PhysicalDesign/Orchestration/`.

## Goal

Treat region shape, cell position, orientation, pin access, channel space, and
interface capacity as one coordinated decision instead of committing placement
before its routing consequences are understood.

## Required behavior

- Maintain a portfolio of placement-and-routing alternatives.
- Allow placement to propose positions, transforms, access alternatives, and
  spatial requirements without committing accepted routing ownership.
- Allow routing to return provisional corridors, route trees, capacity demand,
  scoped conflicts, and placement feedback without mutating placement state.
- Expand only the boundary, channel, or region that evidence identifies as the
  bottleneck.
- Keep locally feasible alternatives until global capacity and interface
  compatibility have been evaluated.
- Compare completed candidates using the shared R9 objective rather than gate
  footprint alone.
- Give the coordinator sole ownership of retries, the incumbent, accepted
  resource ownership, and final candidate commitment.

## Boundary rules

Placement and routing communicate through immutable requests, results, and
feedback records. A blocked pin identifies the affected pin, claims,
reservations, proof scope, and assumptions. Routing does not reach into a
placement context and change coordinates.

## Reuse and stale-result rules

A joint candidate contains separately reviewable claims. Its placement, access,
global-plan, local-route, score, and proof portions are not one indivisible
cache hit.

Matching placement geometry alone cannot preserve a joint candidate when
boundary capacity, corridor demand, external reservations, the redstone model,
or the objective has changed. The coordinator may salvage unaffected subclaims
under [N6](../../N/N6/N6.md) and must recompute the rest.

## Acceptance

- A routing conflict produces scoped feedback and another coordinated
  alternative rather than cross-stage mutation.
- A local bottleneck can enlarge without forcing unrelated regions to expand.
- Candidate ordering uses completed or explicitly classified estimated routed
  metrics.
- Equivalent worker completion orders yield the same deterministic accepted
  result.
